Neutrinos in the beam, gas in the detector.

I am an experimental and phenomenological particle physicist. My PhD tested Lorentz invariance violation in long-baseline neutrino oscillations. Now I build and simulate Spherical Proportional Counters, and I am a member of the NOvA Collaboration.

Research

Gaseous detectors and instrumentation

Simulation-driven design, readout development and experimental characterisation of Spherical Proportional Counters. I wrote their Garfield++ simulation from scratch, including a voxelization-based fast simulation.

Neutrino physics and BSM phenomenology

Oscillations, Lorentz invariance violation and other new-physics effects in long-baseline experiments, linking phenomenological predictions to experimental data.

Experimental particle physics

Detector R&D for large experiments, combining detector development, electronics and data analysis. I also mentor master’s students in detector simulation and development.

The two-flavour oscillation probability, the starting point for what long-baseline experiments like NOvA and DUNE measure:

$$P_{\alpha \to \beta} = \sin^2(2\theta)\,\sin^2\!\left(\frac{\Delta m^2 L}{4E}\right)$$

Lorentz invariance violation adds direction-dependent terms to this picture, which is what the sidereal-effect searches look for.
